How the chamber makes turbulence

  1. Heat. Each heater warms the air in its end compartment. Room air comes in through the perforated end walls. Set one heater lower than the other to get a cooler and a warmer stream.
  2. Collimated jets. Each fan pushes its compartment air through a honeycomb collimator, so it leaves as a straight jet aimed at the centre.
  3. Collision and mixing. The two jets meet head-on in the mixing region. The shear and the temperature contrast break the meeting zone into eddies, so hot and cold pockets end up side by side. Warm air has a lower refractive index, so each pocket delays the light by a different amount. Switch the view to Cn² to see where this is strongest.
  4. Spill-over. The mixed air escapes through the gap into the open section and out of whichever openings are open.
  5. Short vs long path. On the short path the beam runs straight through the mixing region. On the long path it crosses the open section, which only the spilled-over plume reaches. The Taylor fan blows that turbulent air across the beam, so the phase pattern drifts over the aperture as a frozen pattern (Taylor's hypothesis).
  6. Why about 30 % fan speed. With slow jets, the hot air rises toward the lid and leaves the beam height before the streams meet. With fast jets, the fans pull in more room air, which dilutes the temperature contrast. The model peaks near 30 %, which matches your earlier characterization (at least in some cases).

What the model does

  • Airflow: 2-D incompressible flow in the plane of the openings, 6.25 mm grid, with the walls, heaters, fans, vents and openings from the diagram. The fans and heater blowers are fixed-velocity inlets with about 12 % fan turbulence.
  • 3-D effects, simplified: buoyant air leaves the beam plane when jets are slow (Richardson-number weighted), air can leak in or out of the plane (drawn-in air is at room temperature), and friction from floor and lid damps large eddies.
  • Optical turbulence: Cn² = (79×10⁻⁶ P / T²)² CT², with CT² from the simulated temperature structure function at 1.25 cm and P = 888 hPa (Calgary). r₀ = (0.423 k² ∫Cn² dz)−3/5; Rytov variance for a plane wave.
  • Receiver: von Kármán phase screens (L₀ = 8 cm, l₀ = 1 mm) scaled to the simulated r₀, moved by the simulated crosswind and slowly re-randomised by the local eddy speed.
  • Assumptions to check: heater setting = heater outlet air temperature; heater blowers 1.2 m/s; fan 100 % = 7 m/s at the collimator; Taylor fan up to 5 m/s; room at 20 °C. Absolute Cn² values are estimates. Use the calibration slider to match your measurements.
