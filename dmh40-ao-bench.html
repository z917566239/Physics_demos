Why the WFS must be conjugate to the DM

Beams leave the DM at three tilts (the blue one sweeps). The reducer brings all of them back to the same footprint only at the DM image plane (green). The bars beside the WFS compare the sweeping beam's footprint with the untilted one.

L1 and L2 image the DM onto one plane behind L2. Only there does each lenslet see the same patch of the DM for every beam angle, so the slopes the WFS measures are the slopes the DM made.

An error ΔzW at the WFS equals ΔzW/M² at the DM, which is 39× for the 100 → 16 mm pair. Over that distance the beam walks with any tilt, and its phase ripples partly turn into intensity ripples (scintillation). Once the WFS reads a controlled mode with the wrong sign, the integrator pushes that mode further every frame and the loop runs away. Recalibrating at the wrong plane doesn't help, because calibration uses a flat beam and it is the turbulence that scintillates.

The loop holds while the Fresnel scale √(λΔzDM) stays below r₀, so |ΔzW| ≲ M²r₀²/λ. Stronger turbulence tightens this. A longer wavelength (r₀ grows as λ6/5) or a larger M relaxes it.

Scenarios keep the interaction matrix from the image plane, as if the WFS was moved after calibration.

Model notes and assumptions
  • Turbulence (lab chamber). The chamber's path-integrated phase is represented as one equivalent layer: von Kármán statistics with outer scale L₀ = 8 cm and inner scale l₀ = 1 mm (as in the chamber model). The crosswind carries it across the beam (Taylor frozen flow at speed v, fG = 0.427 v/r₀). The chamber is upstream of the FSM and not conjugate to the DM, but the ~0.5 m path to the DM (assumed) is neglected: √(λL) ≈ 0.6 mm is well below r₀, so scintillation at the DM is weak. OPD is achromatic, so the r₀ and fG sliders read at the laser wavelength and move when λ changes. With air crossing the beam at ~1 m/s, fG would be ~200 Hz at r₀ = 2 mm, far beyond what a 190 Hz loop can follow. The default flow is slow so the loop has something to win.
  • FSM (PI S-330). Corrects all tip/tilt, closed on the camera's centroid at 1 kHz (gain slider). Second-order plant, fn = 1.6 kHz, ζ = 0.15 (assumed), ±5 mrad mechanical (10 mrad range), 1″ mirror, 150 mm before the DM (assumed). The DM loop never sees tip/tilt; any tilt the DM happens to make is taken out by the FSM.
  • DM (DMH40, from the manual). Unimorph piezo mirror, Ø18 mm, 17 mm optical aperture, 14 mm pupil. 40 segments: 8 inner sectors, 16 in the second ring (both inside the pupil) and 16 outside the pupil, numbered as in the manual. The inner-ring radius (3.9 mm) is read off the drawing. Each segment's command is (V − 150 V)/150 V, clamped to ±1. The surface comes from a thin plate with a piezo bending moment and a free rim (the 3 mounting joints only fix piston and tilt). It is scaled so all segments at full command give the manual's ±17.6 µm defocus. It reproduces the manual's spherical and secondary-astigmatism strokes and gives about half its astigmatism and coma. Hysteresis (compensated in Thorlabs' driver), creep, the 0.5 ms response and the 200 nm rms open-loop figure are not modelled.
  • WFS (WFS20-5C). 35 × 35 lenslets, 150 µm pitch, lenslet focal length 5.2 mm (assumed), 5 µm pixels. Each spot centroid is the intensity-weighted mean phase gradient over its lenslet, which is exact for the far-field centroid. Dynamic range is p/2 − λf/p, centroid noise ~1/50 pixel at full signal, and lenslets below 5 % of calibration intensity are dropped. The silicon cutoff near 1.1 µm is ignored by default.
  • Reducer and propagation. Thin lenses separated by f₁ + f₂. DM image plane at f₂ + M²(f₁ − d₁) behind L2, plus 3.4 mm for a 10 mm N-BK7 cube. From that plane to the lenslets the field is propagated with a band-limited angular spectrum on a 128² (0.2 mm) or 256² (0.1 mm) grid in DM coordinates. Image inversion by the Keplerian pair is ignored in the displays.
  • DM loop. One WFS sample per frame. The DM updates 1.5 frames later, about 2 frames of total delay. Leaky integrator; zonal reconstructor from push-pull pokes, keeping the strongest N system modes (SVD of the tip/tilt-filtered interaction matrix). The simulation slows itself down when the browser can't keep up; the speed chip shows the actual rate.
  • Camera. Perfect. Far field from a matrix Fourier transform of the residual field; Strehl = on-axis intensity relative to the unaberrated Gaussian beam.
  • Assumed, not from datasheets. Lenslet focal length, FSM damping, component spacings, the plate's Poisson ratio (0.25).
