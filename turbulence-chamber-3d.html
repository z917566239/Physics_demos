What the 3-D view adds

  1. Warm air rises. The hot jet bends up toward the lid and the cooler jet sinks, so the mixing zone is a tilted, three-dimensional sheet rather than a line. Turn buoyancy off in Model settings to compare.
  2. Real heights. Openings sit at mid-height (16 cm), the fans are 12 cm units in the Ø 14 cm holes of the fan panels, and the heaters stand on the floor, so their plumes have to be pulled up into the fans.
  3. Where the air goes. Room air enters through the perforated end walls. Everything the fans push out must leave through the open openings (or the exhaust), which limits how hard the fans can blow.
  4. Windows. Put a window on a beam opening to stop the airflow there while keeping the beam. Watch how the plume and the Cn² profile change.
  5. Taylor fan and exhaust. On the long path, blowing in at the bottom and sucking out at the top gives a clean crossflow over the beam. The wavefront then drifts across the aperture as a frozen pattern.

What the model does

  • Airflow: incompressible 3-D flow on a 1 cm grid (106 × 66 × 35 cells), staggered velocities, MacCormack advection and a red-black SOR pressure solve, all in WebGL2 shaders. Warm air is lifted by Boussinesq buoyancy.
  • Geometry: panels, fan-panel holes, opening positions and lid from the CAD files; end-wall openings, vents, heaters and the lid-centre opening from the diagram, photo and your notes.
  • Fans: 12 × 12 cm collimated jets with about 12 % turbulence. Delivered speed follows a fan curve, U = Uset(1 − Δp/Δpmax), with Δpmax ∝ Uset². The same applies to the Taylor fan and the exhaust.
  • Optical turbulence: Cn² = (79×10⁻⁶ P / T²)² CT², with CT² from the simulated temperature structure function at 1 cm and P = 888 hPa. The beam average uses 7 points across the beam. r₀ and Rytov variance are for a plane wave.
  • Assumptions to check: heater setting = heater outlet air temperature (blowers 1.2 m/s); heaters 12 × 16 × 22 cm; vents Ø 15 cm, 50 % open; fan pressure limit 100 Pa at 100 %; Taylor fan and exhaust up to 5 m/s; room at 20 °C. Absolute Cn² values are estimates.
